B · ad-hoc lookup — the one thing here that is nearly free
Grounding — the split here is unusually sharp Frame B is nearly free. The requirements audit's Y4 found that "scan or type anything, tell me what it is and where it's at" — the single most useful thing a yard worker could have — is already built and working: /api/portal-tag-find (multi-field search) and /api/portal-tag-history (summary + full audit trail). They sit behind require_portal_auth and the WIP_TAG_HISTORY_V1 flag. Opening them to the worker tier is a permissions decision, not a build.
One constraint on doing that: portal-tag-history resolves actor to a worker name, and names are tenant-only PII. A worker-facing variant must drop or pseudonymise that column — which is why frame B shows the movement history with no operator names.
Frame A is the opposite — nothing behind it. Audit Y1–Y3: there is no task entity, no assignment model and no push mechanism anywhere in the system. A supervisor-pushed task list is a new data model, not a new view. §3.8's supervise side (task pool, worker assignment board, drag-to-assign) is the same gap seen from above. Y5: "find where a bin physically is" also has no location field.
Naming overload worth knowing: in production code "yard" means the plant IP geo-fence and the non-SSO worker posture (_ip_allowed), not a job. Yard is not in WORKER_ROLES and has no scan station. Shipping a role called Yard overloads a term that already means something else in the codebase.
