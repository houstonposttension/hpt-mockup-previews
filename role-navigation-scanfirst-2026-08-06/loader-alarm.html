Corrections and restorations on this page 1. Two tiers, not one. Last night's single red screen with three buttons doesn't exist in production or in the locked design. Tier 1 has exactly one action; tier 2 has four.
2. The amber tier was missing entirely. NOT READY is the loader's guard against loading an unfabbed tag — it names the machine the piece is actually at and tells the loader to send it back. It is gated on fabReady(), and it is amber precisely because it is recoverable.
3. Sound and haptics differ per tier. Tier 1 sounds the alarm tone and vibrates hard; tier 2 is silent with a soft double-tap. My draft printed "TONE + VIBRATE" on the bin-switch, which overstated it.
4. Sub-bins are not a wrong bin. Both sides of the comparison are normalised through baseBin(), which strips the .N suffix — so scanning X8.2 while locked to X8 is the same load and raises nothing. That rule is why frame C compares X4 to X8, two different base bins.
5. Combine restored. The fourth action, and the side-by-side bin cards with progress, are the genuinely new capability in the locked design — today's modal shows only the new bin's name, which isn't enough to choose well.
Combine is a real refactor, not a UI toggle. The loader is architected around exactly one load: LOAD is a single object, the 4-part load_key stops identifying the run once two customers are involved, _persistBin() writes one active bin, and /api/load-status returns one load. Three questions are still open and unanswered: is combine reversible mid-run · do the other bin's already-loaded tags survive (the mockup assumes yes) · and the event name should be BINS_COMBINED, matching the registry's SCREAMING_SNAKE past-participle convention.
